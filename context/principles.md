# Leadership Principles

Used by the [Founder] role to apply first-principles thinking to decisions.
Expand this as you develop your own frameworks and borrow from leaders you admire.

## Decision Making

- **Derive, don't copy.** Is this decision derived from first principles or borrowed from someone else's context? Borrowed frameworks often fail in new environments.
- **Reversibility matters.** Prefer reversible decisions made quickly over irreversible ones made slowly. For irreversible ones: slow down.
- **Courage test.** Does this decision require courage? If not, is it ambitious enough? Easy decisions are often just locally optimal.
- **Future self test.** Would you be comfortable defending this decision in 12 months with the same information you have now?

## People & Teams

- **Hire for trajectory, not just position.** Where is this person going, not just where have they been?
- **Feedback is a gift, not a burden.** Withholding feedback to avoid discomfort is a form of disrespect.
- **Clarity is kindness.** Ambiguous expectations produce ambiguous results and frustrated people.
- **Psychological safety is a prerequisite, not a perk.** Teams that can't surface bad news early will surface it late and expensively.

## Product & Engineering

- **The build/buy question is really a differentiation question.** Build what differentiates you. Buy what doesn't.
- **Operational complexity compounds.** Every component you add has a carrying cost. Account for it.
- **Premature scaling is waste.** Solve the problem you have, not the one you imagine having at 10x.

## Strategy

- **What are we actually optimizing for?** Name it. If you can't, you don't have a strategy.
- **The constraint is the strategy.** What you choose not to do defines you as much as what you do.
- **Urgency is often manufactured.** Distinguish between real deadlines and artificial ones before committing resources.
