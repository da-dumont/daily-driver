# First 30 Days at Constant Contact

**Start Date: May 4, 2026**

---

## Pre-Start (Now → May 3)

- [ ] Write down key alignment points from the Shane Franklin conversation while still fresh
- [ ] Research Constant Contact's current public AI surface area — blog posts, product announcements, job listings
- [ ] Identify who the key players are in product and design before day one
- [ ] Review the agentic product strategy notes and federated model framing — internalize it before you're in meetings

---

## Week 1 (May 4–9) — Orient and Listen

- [ ] Meet with Tim Thornton — ask where he thinks AI could change something real for customers, and what's already been tried
- [ ] Meet with Shane Franklin — same question, different angle. Use this to pressure test your pre-start read
- [ ] Meet with Lejin — understand scope, priorities, and how they see the new role fitting in. Build this relationship early
- [ ] Get the lay of the land on existing autonomous team structure — how are KPIs assigned and by whom
- [ ] Don't propose solutions yet

---

## Week 2 (May 11–16) — Go Wider

- [ ] Schedule conversations with support, sales, and customer success leads
- [ ] Sit in on customer calls if possible
- [ ] Start reading existing customer research — look for frustration clusters, not individual quotes
- [ ] Ask each internal team the same question: where do customers give up or work around the product?
- [ ] Assess Constant Contact's API surface area and data platform maturity

---

## Week 3 (May 18–23) — Go Into the Product

- [ ] Use the product like a customer — find what feels slow, manual, or repetitive
- [ ] Find out what AI experiments or prototypes have already been built internally, shipped or not
- [ ] Understand the current data model — how clean is it, how exposed is it, could an agent reason over it
- [ ] Map the existing integration surface — what systems do customers connect CC to today

---

## Week 4 (May 25–30) — Synthesize and Pressure Test

- [ ] Write down the top three problem areas worth betting on
- [ ] Define kill criteria for what a POC would need to show to keep going
- [ ] Take findings back to Shane and Tim as a conversation, not a presentation
- [ ] Draft a rough POC framework — discovery to shaping to running the bet
- [ ] Identify which existing teams are functioning well versus just looking like the new model on paper

---

## Running Notes

- Before pushing for managed Claude agents, assess data foundation readiness first. Agents built on top of messy or incomplete data erode trust fast, and recovering from that is harder than going slower upfront. The data layer needs to be solid enough for an agent to reason over it reliably before any customer-facing or internal agent work gets prioritized.
